Score anatomy

Start at 100. Every violation subtracts.

The portal publishes no usable numeric score. Our 0–100 formula runs on every report to give each inspection a score — no letter. The A–F letter belongs to the facility, not any single visit: a score bands to a grade only once it anchors a facility (see The facility grade, below).

Computed here

Deduction per violation each block is one point off the score

Risk-factor violation Form items 1–29 — the practices most tied to foodborne illness: temperatures, cooking, hygiene, approved sources, contamination.
First time flagged −6
Detected repeat 6 × 1.5 −9
Good-retail-practice violation Form items 30+ — operational upkeep: cleaning, labeling, equipment, maintenance.
First time flagged −2
Detected repeat 2 × 1.5 −3

A risk-factor violation costs three times a good-retail-practice one; a detected repeat costs half again (hollow blocks); a violation corrected on site while the inspector watched earns 25% of its deduction back. The math runs exact and rounds once at the end — halves round up, toward the better score — then floors at 0.

A 70 broad inspection → a B 83 facility grade — visibly better, but not laundered: the calibration keeps a fully-cleared F 0 inside D at best, and no follow-up alone reaches A from D or F. Re-earning the letter takes the next broad inspection.

Exact scoring edge cases

Repeat sourceA structured VDH Repeat flag or the word “repeat” in the observation makes that violation 1.5×.

Missing item numberDefaults to the lower good-retail-practice deduction of 2 points.

Corrected on siteDeducts 75% of its weight — the structured checklist’s COS marking (every OUT row for the item) earns 25% back. The credit is provisional: a later re-check that finds the item OUT again revokes it to the full deduction. It never erases the recorded violation or softens red-flag ranking.

Follow-up reportsChecklist breadth—not the word “follow-up”—decides their role. A broad report publishes a score; focused and scope-unknown reports publish null. Their per-item IN/OUT verdicts can still adjust the facility grade above, joined on the form item number.

Grade without follow-upsIdentical to the latest broad score, banded to a letter. Items never re-checked keep their full deduction; undated or same-day reports never adjust.

Written-verdict follow-upsSome follow-ups publish no checklist at all — the inspector’s verdict lives only in the written comments (“all violations corrected”). Those comments are adjudicated into a machine verdict by a separate, audited step — never parsed loosely at scoring time — and then adjust the grade exactly like a re-check of the items the broad inspection docked: “all corrected” restores like a full re-checked-IN clear, “not corrected” charges the verified-repeat ×1.5. Hedged or ambiguous comments (“corrected or are continuing to be implemented”) grant nothing until reviewed. A written verdict can restore or re-charge existing deductions, never add new ones, and a later structured re-check always outranks an earlier comment.

Field-level boundary

What is official, and what is ours

Color is not the contract. Every group is labeled in words.

Official VDH record

Archived and reorganized

  • Facility name, address, permit type, and status
  • Permit and inspection GUIDs
  • Inspection date, type, and purpose
  • Violation item, citation, observation, and corrective action
  • Checklist IN/OUT/N/A/N/O, COS, and Repeat markings
  • Temperatures and inspector comments when present
CleanPlateVA-derived

Computed or enriched

  • Inspection score: raw 0–100 formula on each report (no letter)
  • Facility grade: score + A–F letter, latest broad ± follow-up re-checks
  • Checklist compliance percentage
  • Broad-only score trend, red flags, and open-repeat count
  • Map coordinates and approximate-location flag
  • “Restaurants only” classification
  • Presentation merge of likely predecessor permits

Public finder

Identity, location, source handoff

Snapshot markers recorded as active at export time, names, addresses, geocoded coordinates, restaurant classification, approximation and mobile-unit flags, permit IDs, and the VDH district route needed for the source handoff. Status can age; presence is not proof a facility is currently open or permitted. No report dates, scores, grades, or inspection content.

Browser tries fullfalls back to finder

Authenticated archive

Reports plus derived signals

Whitelisted histories, citations, observations, checklists, temperatures, comments, status, lineage, and computed metrics from private R2 behind Cloudflare Access.

05

Limits & verification

What this view cannot promise

Transparency includes the edges, not just the happy path.

Selected coverage

Coverage is the ZIP count shown above, not every Virginia jurisdiction. Absence from this map does not mean absence from VDH; some places are outside the selected ZIPs or use other systems.

Snapshot, not live

Collection and publication can lag. “Newest report” is one maximum date, not proof that every covered area is equally current.

Permit status can age

An incremental scan can retain the last known “active” state after a facility stops receiving inspections. A full resweep reconciles closures; a marker is not operating-status proof.

Source retention

The portal typically exposes about two years of history. Missing report pages remain missing or unscored; this is an archive of what was collected, not a complete lifetime record.

Geocoded locations

Pins come from address lookups through Census, VGIN, OpenStreetMap/Nominatim, then ZIP-centroid fallback—not VDH coordinates. Some unbadged pins are street-level or interpolated, not rooftop; centroid fallbacks are labeled approximate.

Presentation heuristics

“Restaurants only” uses permit-type/name patterns. History merging uses shared location/address plus name similarity. Either can misclassify, combine, or miss a match; retained permit IDs are the audit trail.

Focused is not facility-wide

A focused follow-up stays visible as the latest event and adjusts the facility grade item by item, but it never replaces the broad inspection that anchors it. Its own score is null; the OUT/addressed result describes that targeted visit. When comments enumerate corrected items omitted from the structured rows, both channels count once and the structured row governs any conflict.

VDH wins conflicts

Scores and summaries are comparison tools, not safety or illness predictions. If our presentation and the source disagree, the official VDH record is authoritative.

Verify at the source

Facility rows and report cards expose MyHealthDepartment links where available. Compare the observation, citation, corrective action, and checklist markings yourself.
